# Elephant

## Body
The African elephant is the largest land animal in the world. A big male can weigh about 6,000 kilograms, which is as much as several cars. Elephants have thick, wrinkled grey skin, huge ears and a long trunk.

## Trunk and tusks
The trunk is a nose and an upper lip joined together. Elephants use it to breathe, smell, grab food, drink water and hug each other. Tusks are very long teeth that keep growing all through an elephant's life. Elephants use them to dig for water and roots and to move branches out of the way.

## Ears
Elephants flap their big ears to cool down. Blood flows close to the skin of the ears, and the moving air carries heat away on hot days.

## Food and water
Elephants eat only plants: grass, leaves, bark and fruit. An adult can eat well over 100 kilograms of food in one day and can drink about 100 litres of water.

## Family life
Elephants live in family groups called herds. The herd is led by the oldest female, called the matriarch. A mother elephant is pregnant for about 22 months, longer than any other land animal. A newborn calf weighs about 100 kilograms and can stand up within an hour.

## Where they live
African elephants live in savannas and forests in many countries, including Kenya, Botswana and Nigeria, where some live in Yankari Game Reserve.
